   Transaction descriptions in credit card statements are short, non-standardized and noisy: they contain truncated
   abbreviations, payment intermediary prefixes, and errors introduced during document reading. This noise prevents
   the automatic categorization of expenses and, with it, personal budget planning. This monograph designed,
   implemented, and experimentally evaluated an end-to-end computational solution for the extraction, sanitization,
   and automatic classification of expenses in the Brazilian context, executable on local infrastructure and
   compliant with the Brazilian General Data Protection Law. The solution combines document extraction with tabular
   layout preservation, deterministic lexical normalization routines, and an agent orchestrated as a state graph
   that articulates dense vector retrieval, reasoning with a local large language model, autonomous enrichment
   tools, and human confirmation for low-confidence cases, on a Data Lakehouse infrastructure with experiment
   tracking. The evaluation used 699 transactions from real statements, audited against the issuing institution's
   official files, to measure extraction quality, and a set of 1,660 transactions labeled into 11 categories, to
   measure the predictive performance of five architectures under a merchant-disjoint blind
   test. Layout-preserving extraction reduced the character error rate from 52.82\% to
   \textbf{5.72\%} and raised the exact match rate from 3.3\% to 86.2\%. In classification, the sparse representation
   based on character $n$-grams combined with Random Forest achieved the best Macro-F1 (\textbf{69.3\%}, in a configuration without the lexical normalization stage), with no statistically
   significant difference from Naive Bayes (63.6\%; McNemar's test, $p = 0.059$) nor from dense vector retrieval in its best configuration (66.8\%; $p = 0.86$), and ahead of the hybrid agent (60.0\%) and the fine-tuning of a byte-level model (48.3\%);
   the best overall accuracy was \textbf{88.3\%}. No architecture reached the 85\% Macro-F1 threshold
   established in the central hypothesis, which is therefore refuted on the primary metric. The finding
   of widest methodological reach is the gap of 23 to 25 percentage points between conventional cross-validation and the merchant-disjoint
   blind test, which quantifies how much of the apparent performance of this class of classifier stems from
   memorizing previously observed merchants. It is concluded that the proposed pipeline is functionally viable and
   auditable under local hardware constraints, and that its predictive effectiveness remains conditioned on the
   volume and coverage of the labeled corpus.
