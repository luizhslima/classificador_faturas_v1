    Personal financial management faces critical barriers due to the lack of standardization and textual noise present
   in credit card statement descriptions, such as truncated abbreviations, payment gateway prefixes, and optical
   character recognition (OCR) errors. This work presents an end-to-end computational solution for the extraction,
   sanitation, and automatic classification of financial expenses in the Brazilian context, based on Artificial
   Intelligence, Natural Language Processing (NLP), and data engineering. The proposed methodology combines a hybrid
   extraction pipeline with structured layout analysis (IBM Docling V2) and local OCR (Tesseract), lexical sanitation
   routines, and an intelligent agent architecture orchestrated in state graphs (LangGraph). The system integrates
   dense vector search in milliseconds via PostgreSQL with the pgvector extension (RAG), contextual semantic reasoning
   with Large Language Models (LLMs), autonomous web search enrichment tools, and a Human-in-the-Loop mechanism for
   ambiguous transactions, supported by a containerized Data Lakehouse infrastructure and an MLOps pipeline with MLflow,
   MinIO, and Apache Kafka in strict compliance with the LGPD. Experimental validation used the Federal Government Payment Card
   dataset (over 470,000 real transactions) for textual-noise characterization and a labeled set of 1,660 real C6 Bank
   and Nubank statement transactions for predictive measurement, under a strict merchant-disjoint blind-test protocol.
   The structured extractor (IBM Docling V2) reduced the character error rate (CER) from 52.8\% (classical OCR) to
   \textbf{5.72\%}, raising the exact-match rate from 3.3\% to 86.2\%. For semantic classification, the classical
   sparse baselines (TF-IDF with Random Forest) were the most robust, with a Macro F1-Score of \textbf{69.3\%} and
   84.7\% accuracy, ahead of dense vector retrieval (56.2\%), the hybrid LangGraph agent (60.0\% Macro-F1 / 61.4\%
   accuracy, with Human-in-the-Loop on 4.5\% of cases) and ByT5 fine-tuning (48.3\%). A gap of about 23 percentage
   points was observed between conventional cross-validation and the merchant-disjoint blind test, highlighting the
   weight of memorizing previously seen merchants. No model reached the 85\% Macro F1-Score threshold under this
   protocol --- attributed to the small size of the labeled personal dataset and its long category tail --- although
   global accuracy exceeds it. The solution proves the full functional feasibility of modular, reactive and auditable
   AI pipelines under local hardware constraints, and delimits the conditions (labeled-data volume and local-model
   capacity) required for predictive effectiveness at scale.
